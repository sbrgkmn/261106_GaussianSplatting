1. Make a connected capture

Start with 50–250 sharp, overlapping photographs of a manageable facade or building. Walk across and around it. Keep exposure and focal length steady. Connect elevated and ground views with intermediate angles; each added view should share recognizable details with its neighbors. Avoid mixing restoration eras.

2. Align and inspect

Run from this project's PowerShell directory. Replace the example path with your image folder. The alignment-only pass checks that at least 80% of images join the largest reconstruction. Logs and the COLMAP camera model stay under data/reconstructions/.

./.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/reconstruct.py "C:/captures/building" --title "My building" --align-only

3. Train the capture

Use the same folder without --align-only for a fresh alignment and training run. A completed Gaussian PLY appears automatically in Atelier's scene menu after reload. The default is 30,000 training steps and a one-million-splat budget. This is a longer reconstruction job, not the seconds-long quick preview.

./.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/reconstruct.py "C:/captures/building" --title "My building"

Video input is supported through ffmpeg. This example extracts up to 200 frames at one frame per second:

./.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/reconstruct.py "C:/captures/walk.mp4" --title "Facade walk" --fps 1 --max-frames 200

For varied internet photographs, --preset in-the-wild selects the trainer's corresponding preset. Alignment still has to pass. Failed or fragmented alignment is not replaced with an inferred scene.

4. Compose the drawing

Open the trained capture in Atelier. Blend stipples, line connections and the photographic layer. Use the reveal timeline to draw it progressively, then add sound. PNG exports the rendered view; SVG exports monochrome points and connections for further drawing work. Composition JSON saves the camera and visual settings, not the source capture or audio.
